Visiting Shanghai for EPOWER 2027

Last updated 15 October 2026

  • 📅 Dates: 3–5 June 2027
  • 📍 Venue: SNIEC (2027 venue unconfirmed)
  • 🏙️ City: Shanghai, China
  • 🗣️ Local language: Mandarin Chinese

EPOWER is a Shanghai trade fair for electric power equipment and technology, covering generation, transmission, distribution and electrical engineering. The 2027 dates are confirmed. The venue is expected to be the Shanghai New International Expo Centre (SNIEC), but it is not yet confirmed, so check the organiser's site before you book a hotel next to it.

Always confirm opening hours, registration and any last-minute changes on the official event website before you travel. The dates and venue here come from our trade-show list, and we update them when organisers announce changes.

Venue and getting there

If the show is at SNIEC in Pudong, Longyang Road station (Lines 2 and 7, plus the Maglev terminus) and Huamu Road station (Line 7) are the nearest stops.

From Pudong International Airport (PVG), the Maglev reaches Longyang Road in about eight minutes. From Hongqiao Airport or railway station, Line 2 runs east to Longyang Road.

Shanghai's metro is fast, cheap and signed in English. Taxis are metered, but have your destination written in Chinese for the driver.

June is the start of the plum-rain season in Shanghai: warm, humid and often wet. Carry a compact umbrella.

Business etiquette in China

  • Offer and receive business cards with both hands, read the card for a moment, and don't write on it or put it straight in your back pocket. A card with a Chinese side is a real advantage.
  • Address people by surname and title (for example 王总 Wáng zǒng, “Director Wang”) until they invite you to use a first name.
  • WeChat is the main channel for business contacts and follow-up; many visitors share WeChat QR codes rather than email addresses at the stand.
  • Seniority matters: greet the most senior person first and expect the senior person to lead on decisions.
  • At a banquet, wait to be seated, let the host make the first toast, and hold your glass slightly lower than a senior person's when you clink.
  • Mobile payment is everywhere. Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign card before you travel, and carry some cash as a backup.
  • Direct refusals are rare. “We'll study it” (我们研究一下 wǒmen yánjiū yíxià) often means no, or not yet.

Key Mandarin Chinese phrases for the show

English will carry most conversations at an international trade show. Still, opening with a greeting in Mandarin Chinese and knowing how to ask for a stand, a taxi or a slower repeat makes the trip smoother and leaves a good impression.
