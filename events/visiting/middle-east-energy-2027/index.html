Visiting Dubai for Middle East Energy 2027

Last updated 15 October 2026

  • 📅 Dates: 11–13 May 2027
  • 📍 Venue: Dubai Exhibition Centre, Expo City Dubai
  • 🏢 Organiser: Informa Markets
  • 🏙️ City: Dubai, UAE
  • 🗣️ Local language: Arabic (with English widely used in business)

Middle East Energy is the region's long-running exhibition for power generation, transmission and distribution, energy storage and critical power. It attracts utilities, EPC contractors, distributors and manufacturers from across the Middle East, Africa and South Asia. The 2027 edition is at the Dubai Exhibition Centre in Expo City Dubai.

Always confirm opening hours, registration and any last-minute changes on the official event website before you travel. The dates and venue here come from our trade-show list, and we update them when organisers announce changes.

Venue and getting there

Dubai Exhibition Centre is in Expo City Dubai, the former Expo 2020 site in the south of the city. Expo 2020 station, at the end of the Dubai Metro Red Line's Expo branch, serves the site directly.

From Dubai International Airport (DXB), take the Red Line towards Expo 2020 (check the train's destination, as the line splits), or allow about 40 minutes or more by taxi. Al Maktoum International Airport (DWC) is closer to the site, if your flight uses it.

Hotels near Expo City, Dubai South or Jebel Ali shorten the commute. If you stay in Downtown or the Marina, the metro is a predictable option at rush hour.

May is hot in Dubai, with daytime temperatures commonly in the high 30s °C. Plan walks outdoors early or late, and drink plenty of water.

Business etiquette in UAE

  • Greet with a handshake using your right hand, and offer and receive cards, documents and refreshments with the right hand too.
  • Wait for a woman to offer her hand first; a hand on the heart and a nod is a polite alternative.
  • Dress conservatively: a suit or smart business wear, with shoulders and knees covered.
  • Arabic coffee and dates are a sign of hospitality. Accept at least one small cup; a gentle shake of the cup tells the host you've had enough.
  • First meetings are about the relationship. Expect small talk about family, travel and the event before business, and don't rush to the price.
  • Ask before you photograph people, and don't photograph government buildings.
  • The UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday, and many companies finish early on Friday, so plan follow-up meetings for Monday to Thursday.
  • English is the everyday business language. A few words of Arabic are still noticed and appreciated, especially greetings.

Key Arabic phrases for the show

English will carry most conversations at an international trade show. Still, opening with a greeting in Arabic and knowing how to ask for a stand, a taxi or a slower repeat makes the trip smoother and leaves a good impression.
